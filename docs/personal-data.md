# Personal data across the project

**Scope and date.** This is a source/data inspection, not a legal opinion or a finding of compliance. Repository snapshots/schema were inspected on 2026-09-29; official source-rights evidence was fetched on 2026-09-29 UTC. Counts for snapshots describe the files then present and can change when snapshots are rebuilt.

## What the sources establish (and do not)

- **Verified: BOAMP reuse licence, with limits.** DILA's official BOAMP [legal notice](https://www.boamp.fr/pages/mentions-legales/) states “Sauf mention contraire, tous les contenus de ce site sont sous licence etalab-2.0.” The official [data.gouv.fr BOAMP dataset record](https://www.data.gouv.fr/datasets/boamp/) and [API BOAMP record](https://www.data.gouv.fr/datasets/api-boamp/) both identify Licence Ouverte (`fr-lo`). This is positive authoritative evidence for reuse of BOAMP-published dataset/site content under Licence Ouverte 2.0 (attribution and licence conditions apply). The BOAMP API catalogue's own metadata still has `license: null` and `license_url: null`; it is incomplete, not proof that no licence exists. **Scope caveat:** this general notice/dataset label does not establish that third-party material included in a notice or attachment is licensed by DILA, nor answer separate privacy/data-protection questions for contact/person information.
- **Cited statutory publication provisions; independently unverified here.** The Code de la commande publique citations for publication of notices ([R. 2183-1 et seq.](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/section_lc/LEGITEXT000037701019/LEGISCTA000037703443/)) and essential procurement data ([R. 2196-1 et seq.](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/section_lc/LEGITEXT000037701019/LEGISCTA000037703670/)) are relevant references, but Légifrance pages could not be successfully retrieved/read in this investigation. Their exact wording and current status have not been independently verified. Even if confirmed, these publication provisions do not by themselves establish that every personal name, direct contact detail, free-text field, or identifier is legally required to be public; no field-specific legal basis was established here.
- **Verified: API Recherche d’entreprises restrictions and source-data distinction.** Its official [OpenAPI description](https://recherche-entreprises.api.gouv.fr/openapi.json) says non-diffusible businesses and rejected RCS registrations are not accessible; it also says this is not the full Sirene base. The current project selects supplier profiles only when `statut_diffusion = O`. This is an upstream restriction, not proof that identical data in BOAMP/DECP or all derived publication is unrestricted.
- **Verified: limited Annuaire business-data enrichment is under LO 2.0.** OpenAPI `info.license` says MIT, which concerns the API/software description, not business records. The official data.gouv.fr dataset [Données des entreprises utilisées dans l'Annuaire des Entreprises](https://www.data.gouv.fr/datasets/donnees-des-entreprises-utilisees-dans-lannuaire-des-entreprises/) is labelled LO 2.0 (`lov2`), describes data available in the Annuaire and its API, and explicitly lists displayed full name, administrative status, diffusion status, and identifiers. This is positive evidence for this project's limited name/status/identifier enrichment, **not every API field or source**. The project keeps its `statut_diffusion = O` gate. An entity-data licence does not decide personal-data rules where records identify sole traders or individuals.

- **Remaining questions:** DILA should clarify the LO 2.0 scope for third-party notice text/attachments and any applicable exceptions; buyers/publishers should clarify why named contacts/signatories and other person fields are supplied, their correction/removal routes and retention. Further API-owner clarification is about fields beyond the documented name/status/identifier enrichment, if used, and the meaning/coverage of `statut_diffusion = O`—not whether that limited enrichment has dataset-level LO 2.0 evidence. No legal conclusion is made here.

## Repository inventory (no individual values reproduced)

The normalized records largely contain buyer/supplier names and organization identifiers. These are **not necessarily personal data** where they identify a legal entity, but sole traders, named business names, or names of individual suppliers/contacts can identify natural persons. Names were not manually classified person-by-person. String free-text can contain other personal details. Relevant files are publicly served as repository assets, including all raw files listed here; normalization does not remove personal material retained in raw snapshots. Beyond France, the SECOP II normalized and raw extracts include individual/tax identifiers and representative/bank fields; OCDS, Prozorro and TED records may carry party/contact and natural-person details. This inventory describes field/schema risk, not a person-by-person review or jurisdiction-specific legal assessment.

**Text-pattern check, not a person count:** scanning only the raw `donnees` text yielded 11,860 distinct email-shaped strings and 12,990 distinct French phone-shaped strings in the 8,386-record BOAMP snapshot; the 14-record consultation snapshot yielded 8 and 11. These are regex matches, include possible organizational/general contacts and false positives, and are not counts of people or confirmed valid contacts. Labels for contact/signatory fields also occur in the notices. No individual values are reproduced here. This demonstrates why treating raw notice text as contact-free would be unsafe.

### Field-level practical actions

1. **Keep the distinctions visible.** In user-facing descriptions and exports distinguish entity names/IDs from personal names/contact details; do not describe all source records as company-only. Do not label API service MIT as a data licence.
2. **Review raw publication separately from the normalized index.** The raw BOAMP files and `data/tours-notices/raw/` are directly fetchable. Before future republishing, establish whether retaining whole notice `donnees`/XML and contact details is necessary and permitted. Consider excluding/minimizing personal direct-contact fields in a future curated export while retaining the source link and notice identifier; retain an auditable source snapshot only if a verified purpose and rights basis supports it. This is a recommendation, not an assertion that the current files are unlawful.
3. **Preserve safeguards on enrichment.** Keep the diffusion-status gate, provenance and retrieval timestamps; do not add non-diffusible API results or treat the availability of an identifier/name in a procurement notice as permission to enrich it from elsewhere.
4. **Targeted upstream follow-up:** ask DILA about third-party notice text/attachments, exceptions, and publication basis for named contacts/representatives; ask the API owner about fields beyond the documented name/status/identifier enrichment (if used) and the meaning/coverage of `statut_diffusion = O`; ask publishing buyers about notice fields that trigger personal disclosures and correction/removal channels.

**Why is it public?** Colombia's official [Ley 1712 de 2014, article 10](https://www.funcionpublica.gov.co/eva/gestornormativo/norma.php?i=56882) directs publication of ongoing state-contracting information and a SECOP link; [Decreto 1082 de 2015, article 2.2.1.1.1.7.1](https://www.funcionpublica.gov.co/eva/gestornormativo/norma.php?i=77653) requires publication in SECOP of process documents and administrative acts, subject to its terms. Colombia Compra Eficiente's [transparency portal](https://www.colombiacompra.gov.co/transparencia) lists contracting and open data. These explain the general publication context, but do not establish that each identity number, contact or bank field must be public.

For France, BOAMP is an official public-notice service; no field-specific requirement to publish every named contact was established here. CNIL guidance on [internet publication and reuse](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/ouverture-et-reutilisation-de-donnees-personnelles-sur-internet-la-cnil-publie-ses-recommandations) and [reuse of disseminated data](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/comment-reutiliser-les-donnees-diffusees) explains that public availability does not remove data-protection obligations. This is French/EU guidance, not a Colombian legal basis or a field-specific BOAMP ruling.

Run `python tools/audit-personal-data.py` from the repository root to produce aggregate text field-name/presence indicators for these non-French datasets. It never prints field values; field-name matching is heuristic and counts occurrences, not people. `nonempty` includes any nonblank value; `meaningful` excludes empty values and recognized placeholder labels (including “No Definido” and “No aplica”), but does not validate content. Outputs are not a substitute for content review and do not scan binary attachments or every XML field.

## Portugal BASE private research inspection (2026-09-30)

The new national BASE archives remain in a private cache outside Git and the website; they are **not** the existing published TED cohorts. A bounded inspection of the first three records/year (nine total, 64 KiB/member) observed buyer, holder and competitor lists containing **NIF/name-shaped strings**. These may identify individuals or sole traders; no person-by-person classification or whole-file population count was performed. No sampled identifiers or names were retained publicly.

The official dataset category is **Other (Public Domain)**, not privacy clearance. BASE explains that contract, modification and execution information is published from reports supplied by buyers/platforms; that general reporting purpose does not establish the necessity of every personal tax ID or unsuccessful bidder name. Some portal explanations are dated 2022 and cite 2018/2019 provisions, so current field-specific legal authority remains unverified.

The implemented candidate importer retains published supplier names as contract-participant names—including individual/sole-trader names where appropriate—without inferring entity type or suspicion. It strips supplier NIF prefixes, omits identified unsuccessful competitors, and requires review of the minimized names/subject text before public redistribution. This is not blanket anonymization of public names. Raw archives remain private with retention review. Schema observations, source statements, official correction/support channels and unanswered questions are in [portugal-base.md](portugal-base.md) and [`../data/portugal-base-rights.json`](../data/portugal-base-rights.json). No source inquiries have been sent.
